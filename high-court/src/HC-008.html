Tamil Nadu Power Producers Association v. Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission

Result
Appeal partly allowed. The impugned order dated 28.01.2020 passed by TNERC in R.A. No. 7 of 2019 is set aside to the extent of the Tribunal’s findings on seven issues: (1) TANGEDCO may collect and verify data but adjudication rests with TNERC; (2) open access shall be granted per Regulations 2014, not subject to captive-status pre-verification; (3) SPV and AOP are distinct—proportionality test does not apply to SPV; (4) verification of CGP status shall be annual, at year-end; (5) no liability for CSS from non-complying users if aggregate 26%/51% thresholds met; (6) no retrospective application of the procedure; (7) weighted-average shareholding concept set aside. No coercive action pending final determination by the Commission.

Point for Determination

  1. Whether the appointment of TANGEDCO (the Distribution Licensee) as both verifying and adjudicating authority for captive status is justified in law, given it stands to benefit from denial of captive status and receipt of Cross-Subsidy Surcharge (CSS)
  2. Whether documents required for grant of open access under Section 9 of the Act can be mandatorily linked to proof of captive status under Rule 3, and whether the prior verification of captive status is a condition precedent for open access
  3. Whether a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) setting up a Captive Generating Plant can be equated with an Association of Persons (AOP) for purposes of applying the proportionality test to consumption
  4. Whether the State Commission is justified in implementing a draft amendment to Electricity Rules 2005 (not yet notified by the Ministry of Power) as the basis for the verification procedure
  5. Whether the criteria and methodology for verifying the twin consumption requirement (minimum 51% of generation on annual basis) is compliant with Rule 3, and whether verification can be conducted at points other than financial year-end
  6. Whether retrospective applicability of the new procedure from financial year 2014-15 is permissible under the Electricity Act 2003
  7. Whether the methodology for verification in the event of change in shareholding or consumption (including the concept of weighted-average shareholding) is in accordance with law and Rule 3

Facts

On 15–31 March and 7–15 April 2017, TANGEDCO issued Circular Memoranda requiring captive generators and users to furnish documents for verification of captive status under Rule 3 of the Electricity Rules 2005. Writ Petitions challenging these circulars were filed before the Madras High Court (Madurai Bench), disposed of on 25 May 2017. TANGEDCO appealed, and the Division Bench (9 October 2018) left the jurisdiction to verify and determine CGP status open for decision by TNERC, directing TNERC to frame a procedure but leaving the appointment of the verifying body open.

On 27 February 2019, TNERC posted a draft procedure for CGP/captive-user verification by TANGEDCO/Distribution Licensee. After a revised draft (9 December 2019) and a hearing (23 December 2019), TNERC on 28 January 2020 passed the impugned order prescribing a detailed procedure vesting verification authority in TANGEDCO and mandating production of documents for captive status as a pre-condition for grant of open access, measured at multiple points during the financial year with weighted-average shareholding calculations.

The TNPPA (appellant) has approximately 7000–10000 captive users in the state, including wind-energy generators. It challenged the impugned order before APTEL, arguing: (a) TANGEDCO is judge in its own cause (beneficiary of CSS if captive status denied); (b) open access cannot be hinged on prior captive-status verification; (c) SPV cannot be treated as AOP; (d) the procedure wrongly applies draft amendments; (e) verification must occur annually at year-end, not mid-year; (f) retrospective applicability is impermissible.

TNERC and TANGEDCO argued the procedure was directed by the High Court, protects the concession, ensures only qualified entities receive CSS exemptions, and that verification at multiple points prevents gaming. The respondent associations (captive users) largely adopted the appellant's arguments. The Supreme Court, on 9 September 2020, directed APTEL to proceed with final hearing.

Reasoning of the Court

Headnote In a captive-generation verification framework, a Distribution Licensee (who stands to receive CSS if captive status is denied) cannot be vested with adjudicatory power, though it may collect and verify data. The State Commission alone holds jurisdiction to adjudicate disputes and determine captive status under Section 86(1)(f). Open access is an independent statutory right under Section 9, not contingent on prior captive-status determination; documents required for open access are governed by the Open Access Regulations 2014, not Rule 3 verification prerequisites. A Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) is a distinct entity from an Association of Persons (AOP) under Rule 3; the proportionality test and proviso thereto apply only to AOPs. Verification of the twin Rule 3 conditions (26% minimum ownership, 51% minimum consumption) must occur annually, at financial year-end, not mid-year or at multiple periods. Individual non-complying shareholders do not forfeit the CGP status of the collective if the aggregate thresholds are met. Delegated legislation cannot have retrospective effect unless the parent Act permits. The concept of weighted-average shareholding is not prescribed in Rule 3.

The verifying authority cannot adjudicate when it benefits from an adverse finding. Although Section 97 permits delegation of regulatory functions by the Commission, the delegation must preserve transparency, unbiasedness and fair play. TANGEDCO, as the sole beneficiary of CSS if a CGP loses its status, cannot be vested with the power to adjudicate captive status—that violates the principle that no person can be a judge in his own cause. The Commission may, under Section 97, delegate the collection and verification of data to TANGEDCO, but adjudication and final determination of captive status must remain with the Commission under Section 86(1)(f). The High Court order of 9 October 2018 left the appointment open for the Commission to decide; it was an interim arrangement, not a final direction. ¶ 10.9–10.17

Open access is a statutory right, not conditional on prior captive-status proof. Under Sections 2(47), 9, 42(2) and the TNERC Open Access Regulations 2014, open access is to be granted as a matter of right, on payment of wheeling charges. Rule 3 deals with conditions for captive status (for CSS exemption); it does not govern the grant of open access. The Regulations 2014 (Regulations 9(2), 13(2)(a), 14(2)(a)) specify only that applications contain details of the entity, quantum, and point of injection/drawl—they nowhere mandate verification of ownership as a pre-condition. Regulation 16(2)(b) limits the distribution licensee's grounds for withholding consent to three factors: metering infrastructure, distribution-system capacity, and RTU/communication facility. By linking open access approval to prior captive-status verification and imposing 30–45-day delays for document submission, the impugned order violates Section 42(3) (non-discrimination) and invents a requirement absent from the Regulations. ¶ 11.18–11.26

SPV and AOP are distinct entities; the proportionality proviso does not apply to SPVs. Rule 3(1)(a) prescribes the general conditions for CGPs and contains two provisos: one for cooperative societies, one for Associations of Persons (AOP). Rule 3(1)(b) separately addresses SPVs and states that the conditions in Rule 3(1)(a)(i) and (ii) apply but does not mention the provisos. The Parliament deliberately created an intelligible differentia between SPV (a company incorporated for a specific purpose) and AOP (an unincorporated entity). An AOP is a recognized tax entity and, once incorporated, becomes a company; the concept of 'association' implies an unincorporated joint enterprise. The provisos are exceptions to the general rule and cannot travel beyond the provision to which they attach. The second proviso to Rule 3(1)(a)—mandating that captive users holding aggregate 26% ownership must consume electricity in proportion to their shares, within ±10%—applies only to AOPs and cannot be read into Rule 3(1)(b) governing SPVs. The Tribunal’s decision in Kadodara (which equated SPV to AOP for proportionality purposes) is held to be per incuriam for having ignored the statutory distinction, the tax-law concept of AOP as unincorporated, and foundational cases on the meaning of 'association of persons'. ¶ 12.13–12.19

Verification of captive status is an annual exercise, conducted at the end of the financial year. Rule 3 mandates that the twin conditions—26% minimum ownership and 51% minimum annual consumption—be determined on an annual basis. In Prism Cement (Appeal Nos. 2 & 179 of 2018), this Tribunal held that both conditions must be verified together at year-end. Rule 3 does not require pre-verification of shareholding before grant of open access; it prescribes annual verification at the end of the financial year to determine whether the CGP qualifies for CSS exemption. If the Distribution Licensee delays or denies open access in order to block verification and assessment of captive status, it cannot benefit from its own default by claiming CSS at year-end. The impugned order’s direction to furnish ownership details 30–45 days before the date of open access, and to report shareholding changes within 10 days, contradicts Rule 3’s annual framework and creates undue burden. Verification is an independent exercise and must be done at year-end, not at multiple points during the year. ¶ 11.19–11.22

Aggregate compliance with the 26%/51% thresholds is the operative test; individual non-compliance does not nullify collective status. Per this Tribunal’s judgments in Appeal No. 252 of 2014 (MSEDC v. MERC) and Appeal No. 316 of 2013 (Sai Wardha), once a set of captive users collectively hold minimum 26% ownership and collectively consume minimum 51% of generation, the collective qualifies as captive. An individual shareholder who fails the proportionality test (where applicable, as in AOP) loses the benefit of CSS exemption, but that does not strip the collective or other complying members of captive status. Rule 3(2) provides that if the minimum percentage of captive use is not met in any year, the entire generation is treated as non-captive supply—but this refers to the aggregate. The direction in the impugned order (Para 7.8.2) that non-complying consumers trigger CSS levy on remaining complying consumers is contrary to Rule 3 and the Tribunal’s settled jurisprudence. ¶ 14.6–14.7

Delegated legislation cannot have retrospective effect absent express grant in the parent Act. The Electricity Act 2003 contains no provision permitting retrospective effect to delegated legislation. Under settled principle, a delegated authority may exercise only those powers expressly conferred; the legislature alone has plenary power to legislate retrospectively. The impugned order directs the procedure to apply retrospectively from financial year 2014-15, requiring CGPs to furnish documents for verification of years already concluded and transactions already settled (open access already granted). Such retrospectivity amounts to changing the rules of the game after the game was played, contrary to the Supreme Court’s ruling in K. Manjusree v. State of Andhra Pradesh. However, the distribution licensee may, for past years, verify captive status on the basis of data already furnished by the CGP/user while availing open access. ¶ 15.5–15.8

Weighted-average shareholding is not prescribed in Rule 3; verification is on year-end shareholding. The impugned order directs that if shareholding changes during the year, the distribution licensee shall verify on a weighted-average basis. Weighted-average shareholding is not envisaged in Rule 3. Shareholding in captive structures is fluid and dynamic; allowing mid-year reallocation on a weighted-average basis would create unpredictable difficulties for the CGP and shareholders. Verification must be done on the shareholding existing at the end of the financial year, without application of weighted-average calculations at interim points. The Tribunal’s decision in Prism Cement supports annual, year-end verification, and the concept of weighted-average shareholding remains pending adjudication before the Tribunal in other appeals (A. No. 340 & 341 of 2018), hence cannot be applied now. ¶ 16.6–16.8

Interpretation of the Electricity Statutes

Electricity Act, 2003 & Rules

Section 9 — Right to Open Access for Captive Use

Section 9 of the Act grants a person who has established a captive generating plant an unequivocal right to open access for carrying electricity to the destination of the person’s own use. This is independent of, and prior to, the determination of captive status for CSS exemption purposes. Open access is a statutory right to be granted, as a matter of right, on payment of wheeling charges; it is not conditioned on prior proof of captive status. ¶ 10.28, 11.11

Section 42(2) — Open Access and Cross-Subsidy Surcharge Exemption

Section 42(2) mandates that open access be allowed on payment of wheeling and other charges. The section specifically exempts a person who has established a captive generating plant, for carrying electricity to the destination of the person’s own use, from payment of cross-subsidy surcharge (CSS). The exemption is conditioned on satisfaction of Rule 3 criteria, to be verified after open access is exercised, at the end of the financial year. Open access itself cannot be made dependent on such verification. ¶ 10.28, 11.24–11.25

Section 97 — Delegation of Functions by the State Commission

Section 97 permits the appropriate Commission to delegate such of its powers and functions as it thinks fit, except the powers to adjudicate disputes under Sections 79 & 86 and the power to make Regulations under Sections 178 or 181. Any delegation must preserve transparency, unbiasedness and fair play. Delegation to an authority with vested interest in the outcome is impermissible. ¶ 10.15

Rule 3(1)(a) — Captive Generating Plant: General Requirements

Rule 3(1)(a) prescribes two conditions: (i) not less than 26% ownership held by captive user(s), and (ii) not less than 51% of aggregate generation consumed for captive use, determined on an annual basis. These conditions are co-dependent and must be verified together, annually, at the end of the financial year. The Rule does not prescribe the manner or method of verification before open access; it establishes the criteria by which, at year-end, the CGP’s exemption status is determined. ¶ 11.18–11.22

Rule 3(1)(a), Second Proviso — Association of Persons: Proportionality Requirement

In the case of an Association of Persons, the captive user(s) shall hold not less than 26% ownership in aggregate and shall consume not less than 51% of electricity generated, in proportion to their ownership of the power plant within a variation not exceeding ten percent. This proviso is a stand-alone provision applying only to AOPs. It does not apply to CGPs set up by SPVs, which are distinct entities under Rule 3(1)(b). ¶ 12.13–12.14

Rule 3(1)(b) — Special Purpose Vehicle: Applicable Conditions

Where a generating station is owned by a company formed as a special purpose vehicle, the unit(s) identified for captive use must satisfy Rule 3(1)(a)(i) and (ii)—the 26% ownership and 51% consumption thresholds—without reference to the provisos to Rule 3(1)(a). The proportionality test and weighted-average shareholding concepts do not apply to SPVs. ¶ 12.14

Rule 3(2) — Consequence of Non-Compliance with Annual Requirements

If the minimum percentage of captive use (51% of annual aggregate generation) is not complied with in any year, the entire electricity generated shall be treated as if it is a supply of electricity by a generating company. However, this applies to the collective; if an aggregate of 26% ownership and 51% consumption is maintained by the qualifying set of captive users, non-compliance by an individual shareholder does not render the entire generation non-captive. ¶ 14.6

TNERC Grid Connectivity & Intra-State Open Access Regulations 2014, Regulation 9(2) — Eligibility for Open Access

Generating stations, captive generating plants and consumers are eligible for open access to the distribution system on payment of wheeling and other charges. The Regulation mandates non-discriminatory treatment and establishes open access as a statutory entitlement, not dependent on prior verification of the applicant’s captive or non-captive status. ¶ 11.24

TNERC Open Access Regulations 2014, Regulation 16(2)(b) — Conditions for Licensee Consent to Open Access

The distribution licensee may grant or withhold consent only after considering the following factors: (i) existence of infrastructure for time-block-wise energy metering and accounting; (ii) availability of capacity in the distribution network; and (iii) availability of RTU and communication facility. No other document or information may be mandatorily required by the distribution licensee as a condition of open access. ¶ 11.25–11.26

Ratio Decidendi A State Electricity Regulatory Commission may delegate the collection and verification of data regarding captive-plant ownership and consumption to the Distribution Licensee under Section 97, but cannot delegate the adjudicatory function of determining captive status to an entity that stands to benefit financially from a denial of captive status. The determination of captive status under Rule 3—and any consequential levy of cross-subsidy surcharge—is an exclusive function of the State Commission, exercisable under Section 86(1)(f). Open access under Section 9 and Sections 42(2)–(3) of the Act is an independent statutory right governed by the Open Access Regulations 2014; it cannot be made conditional on prior verification of captive status. Verification of the twin Rule 3 conditions (minimum 26% ownership and minimum 51% annual consumption) is mandated by law to occur once yearly, at the end of the financial year, and not at multiple points during the year or on a weighted-average basis. A SPV is distinct from an AOP; the proportionality test applicable to AOPs under the second proviso to Rule 3(1)(a) does not apply to SPVs under Rule 3(1)(b). Delegated legislation cannot have retrospective effect unless the parent Act expressly permits. (The substantive framework on captive-generation requirements and CSS exemption is statutory; the Tribunal’s contribution is on the limits of regulatory delegation and the sequence of verification and open-access grant.)

Also Held & Obiter

  • The High Court’s order of 9 October 2018 left the appointment open; it was not a final directive. Although the Madras High Court (Madurai Bench) in paragraph 10(v) of the order dated 9.10.2018 recorded that TANGEDCO could ‘make a determination’ on receipt of verification particulars, paragraph 10(i) explicitly stated that the issue of jurisdiction and power to verify and determine CGP status was ‘left open’. The direction to TANGEDCO was interim, pending the Commission’s independent application of its mind. Reliance on selective paragraphs of a High Court order, divorced from the full context and the express statement that the issue was left open, is an error in reading judgments.
  • The Tribunal’s prior judgment in Kadodara (on SPV and AOP equivalence) is held per incuriam and does not bind. The Tribunal’s decision in Kadodara (Appeal Nos. 171, 172, 10 of 2008, decided 22.09.2009), treating a SPV as an AOP and applying the proportionality test, was rendered in ignorance of the plain language of Rule 3(1)(b), the distinct nature of a SPV as an incorporated entity versus an AOP as an unincorporated entity, and foundational tax-law cases (B.N. Elias, CIT v. Laxmidas Devidas, Dwaraknath Harishchandra Pitale, Ramanlal Bhailal Patel, CIT v. Buldana Distt., Mohd. Noorulla v. CIT) defining 'association of persons' as an unincorporated joint enterprise. The decision is therefore per incuriam and does not furnish binding authority for equating SPV and AOP.
  • The Tribunal’s appeal decisions citing Kadodara do not overcome the per incuriam holding. While subsequent Tribunal decisions (Prism Cement in Appeal Nos. 2 & 179 of 2018; JSW Steel in Appeal No. 311 of 2018; Sai Wardha in Appeal No. 316 of 2013) have cited Kadodara, those citations do not cure the infirmity in Kadodara itself. The later decisions are secondary authorities relying on a flawed foundation and do not bind a co-ordinate bench deliberating the foundational question afresh.
Disposition The appeal is partly allowed. The impugned order dated 28 January 2020 passed by the Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission in R.A. No. 7 of 2019 is set aside to the extent of the Tribunal’s findings and directions as indicated in paragraphs 17.1 to 17.7 of this judgment, specifically: (1) TANGEDCO may collect and verify data only; adjudication of captive status rests with TNERC; (2) documents for open access shall be those specified in TNERC Open Access Regulations 2014, without additional pre-verification of captive status; (3) SPV and AOP are distinct; proportionality does not apply to SPV; (4) verification of CGP status shall be on an annual basis at the end of the financial year; (5) directions in paras 6.6.3 and 7.8.2 (on CSS levy for individual non-compliance) are set aside; (6) the procedure shall not have retrospective effect from FY 2014-15 (though the Licensee may verify past data already furnished); (7) direction in para 7.6.9 (on weighted-average shareholding) is set aside. The relief sought in Interim Applications Nos. 425, 426, 1210 & 1215 of 2020 does not survive for consideration and is disposed of. No order as to costs.

Significance

This judgment clarifies the division of labour in captive-generation verification: the regulator adjudicates, the licensee collects data. Five key holdings shape the captive-generation ecosystem:

  • A Distribution Licensee cannot adjudicate the very status that exempts it from CSS revenue. Vesting verification and adjudication in the entity with a financial interest in denial violates the principle that no person can be judge in his own cause (Articles 14 and 20 of the Constitution; Section 86(1)(f) as the exclusive locus for determination). The judgment enforces institutional impartiality.
  • Open access is prior to and independent of captive-status verification. Section 9 grants an unconditional right to open access; Rule 3 prescribes the conditions for CSS exemption. By unlinking them, the judgment prevents the Licensee from using open-access approval as leverage to force captive-status documentation, and prevents delays in exercising a statutory right.
  • A SPV is not an AOP; proportionality is not a blanket requirement. By holding Kadodara per incuriam, the Tribunal removes a bar on SPVs. SPVs can now seek and maintain captive status without meeting the proportionality standard (51÷26=1.96% per 1% shareholding), reducing operational rigidity and accommodating corporate structures that use SPVs for project financing.
  • Verification is annual, at year-end, not at multiple points during the year. This aligns verification timing with the statutory framework in Rule 3 and prevents mid-year disruption of open-access arrangements and constant document submission. CGPs can forecast their compliance based on year-end position, not on interim shareholding changes.
  • Retrospective procedures are void; delegated legislation must be prospective. Absent express grant in the parent Act, delegated authorities cannot impose new rules on settled transactions. The judgment protects entities that have already availed open access and furnished documents, insulating those transactions from subsequent re-verification demands.

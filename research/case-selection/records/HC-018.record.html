Assessing Officer-cum-Executive Engineer (Electrical), WESCO v. Appellate Authority-cum-Electrical Inspector

AIR 2018 (NOC) ... ; W.P.(C) No. 13047 of 2015 (Orissa HC) (2017) (AFR)
High Court of Orissa (HC-SB)  |  2017-07-13  |  Bench: Dr. B.R. Sarangi J (1)  |  IK doc 167917487  |  HC-018  |  schema 1.0
A consumer's Section 127 appeal against a Section 126 assessment is not time-barred where the delay is covered by time spent bona fide prosecuting a writ petition/appeal in the wrong forum — Section 14 of the Limitation Act applies (the Electricity Act being a special law under Section 29(2)) — and Section 125 (appeal to the Supreme Court from the Appellate Tribunal) is distinct from the Section 127 assessment appeal; a Section 126 inspection alleging unauthorised use should be conducted in the consumer's presence.

Facts & profile

A power-intensive industry (contract demand 5700 KVA) under WESCO was inspected at night (11.12.2010) and found abstracting power from the 33 KV line by illegal/unauthorised means; a final Section 126 assessment of Rs 2.13 crore followed and supply was disconnected. After the consumer's earlier writ/writ-appeal were held non-maintainable (relegated to a Section 127 appeal), it appealed under Section 127; the Appellate Authority reduced the assessment to Rs 31.01 lakh (unauthorised use for only two months). WESCO challenged that order, contending the appeal was barred by limitation.

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126, s.126(5), s.127, s.135 (contrast), s.125 (contrast), Limitation Act 1963, ss.14, 29(2)

s.127ratiopost-2007 appeal-limitation-s14-exclusion §127 appeal
A Section 127 appeal is not barred by limitation where the delay is attributable to the consumer's bona fide pursuit of a writ petition and writ appeal in the wrong forum; Section 14 of the Limitation Act applies to exclude that period (the Electricity Act being a special law under Section 29(2) that does not expressly exclude Section 14).
“the petitioner is entitled to avail the benefit of Section 14 of the Limitation Act to exempt the period covered by bona fide litigious activity.”— para 14
s.127 / s.125ratiopost-2007 s125-vs-s127-distinction §127 appeal
Section 125 (appeal to the Supreme Court from the Appellate Tribunal, Part XI) and Section 127 (appeal to the appellate authority from a Section 126 assessment, Part XII) are totally different and operate in different contexts; authority on Section 125 limitation does not govern a Section 127 appeal.
“On perusal of the provisions contained in Sections 125 and 127 of the Electricity Act, it appears that they are totally different from each other and considered on different context altogether.”— para 10
s.126(1)ratiopost-2007 inspection-in-consumer-presence natural justice §126 assessment
An inspection alleging unauthorised use under Section 126 should be conducted in the presence of the consumer (a signatory to the inspection report); the licensee's failure to show the night inspection was in the consumer's presence undermined its case.
“if opposite party no.2 was using electricity unauthorizedly and an inspection was caused at 11 PM on 11.12.2010, the same should have been done in presence of the consumer”— para 17
s.126(5)ratiopost-2007 period-of-assessment-126(5) §126 assessment
The Section 126(5) 12-month cap applies only where the period of unauthorised use is not ascertainable; where the period is ascertained (as here, and reduced by the Appellate Authority), the assessment is confined to that ascertained period.
“here is a case where unauthorized use of power has been ascertained by the authority concerned, while adjudicating the matter and determined the liability of consumer-opposite party no.2, and in appeal itself the appellate authority reduced the”— para 19

Ratio & what was decided

§127 appeal HC-SB (Orissa)settles
The limitation for a Section 127 appeal against a Section 126 assessment is subject to Section 14 of the Limitation Act (Electricity Act being a special law under Section 29(2)): time spent bona fide pursuing a writ remedy in the wrong forum is excluded, so the appeal is not time-barred; Section 125 (SC appeal from the Appellate Tribunal) is distinct and its limitation authority does not apply.
§126 assessment HC-SB (Orissa)affirms
A Section 126 inspection alleging unauthorised use should be conducted in the consumer's presence; and the Section 126(5) 12-month cap applies only where the period of unauthorised use is unascertainable.

Significance

An Orissa (AFR) authority on the procedural frame of the Section 126/127 channel: it holds that the limitation for a Section 127 appeal is softened by Section 14 of the Limitation Act (excluding time spent in a wrong-forum writ), distinguishes Section 125 (SC appeal from the Appellate Tribunal) from the Section 127 assessment appeal, insists that a Section 126 inspection be in the consumer's presence, and reads the Section 126(5) 12-month cap as a fallback only where the period is unascertainable. Applies Seetaram Rice Mill on the 'means'/unauthorised-use construction. Topical replacement for the rejected off-topic HC-015.

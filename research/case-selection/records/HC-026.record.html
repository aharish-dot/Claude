V. Swaminathan v. Superintending Engineer, TANGEDCO

W.P.(MD) No. 113 of 2012 (Madras HC, Madurai Bench) (2018)
High Court of Madras (Madurai Bench) (HC-SB)  |  2018-02-09  |  Bench: R. Mahadevan J (1)  |  IK doc 197023991  |  HC-026  |  schema 1.0

Facts & profile

An agriculturist's agricultural service connection was found used for commercial fish farming; the officer assessed the loss at Rs 59,500 and collected Rs 12,000 as compounding fee. The consumer's Section 127 appeal was returned by the Superintending Engineer on the ground that 'no appeal will lie against theft of energy', without a hearing.

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126, s.127, s.135, s.152, s.153

s.152ratiopost-2007 no-compounding-under-protest §152 compounding
Compounding of an offence under Section 152 cannot be done 'under protest' — it is not recognised in law; if the consumer protests, the authorised officer must instead launch prosecution.
“there is no question of contending that the compounding of the offence was under protest, because compounding under protest is not recognised in law. An offence cannot be permitted to be compounded under protest.”— para 22 (adopting Fiem Industries, para 57)
s.152ratiopost-2007 compounding-is-admission-no-retraction §152 compounding
Once the consumer exercises the option to compound (an admission of guilt deemed an acquittal), there is no retracting it and he cannot canvass the correctness of the finding of guilt or claim he was compelled to compound under protest.
“If there is a case of compounding, then the Consumer should not be permitted to canvass on the correctness of the finding of guilt or on the ground that he was compelled to compound under protest. Once option is exercised by the consumer to compound the offence, there is no question of retracting the same.”— para 22 (adopting Fiem Industries, para 61)
s.152 / s.135ratiopost-2007 quantum-of-civil-liability-testable §152 compounding §127 appeal
Even a consumer who has compounded and paid the compounding fee should be permitted to test the correctness of the manner of computation of the civil liability fastened on him; there is a casus omissus (no forum to test the final Section 135 assessment).
“the consumer, who has avoided prosecution, by compounding the offence, paid the compounding fee, should also be permitted to test the correctness of the manner of computation of the civil liability, when such civil liability is fastened on the consumer by the assessing officer.”— para 22 (adopting Fiem Industries, para 64)
s.126 / s.135explanatorypost-2007 126-civil-appealable-vs-135-criminal §126 vs §135 §135 mens rea
Section 135 theft falls within criminal jurisprudence and mens rea is the relevant factor; Section 126 (read with the Supply Code) does not speak of criminal intent, and the assessment under it is appealable under Section 127, whereas a Section 135 assessment (Reg. 23-AA) has no statutory appeal.
“This squarely falls within the dimensions of Criminal Jurisprudence and mens rea is the relevant factor for rendering a finding on theft. On the contrary, Section 126 of the 2003 Act read with Regulation 19 of the Tamil Nadu Electricity Supply Code, does not speak of any criminal intent.”— para 22 (adopting Fiem Industries, para 58)
s.127ratiopost-2007 appeal-rejection-needs-hearing §127 appeal natural justice
An appellate order rejecting a Section 127 appeal as not maintainable, passed without affording any opportunity of hearing, warrants interference and is quashed and remitted.
“the impugned order has been passed by the first respondent by merely stating that the appeal is not maintainable without affording any opportunity of hearing to the petitioner and thus, on that ground alone, the impugned order warrants interference.”— para 23

Ratio & what was decided

§152 compounding HC-SB (Madras)settles
Compounding under Section 152 is an admission of guilt (deemed acquittal) and cannot be made under protest; the consumer who compounds cannot reopen the finding of guilt, but he may test the manner of computation of the civil liability, and an appeal cannot be rejected without a hearing.

Significance

A Madras (Madurai Bench) authority on Section 152 compounding, adopting Fiem Industries: compounding is an admission of guilt deemed an acquittal and cannot be done 'under protest' (the officer must prosecute instead), and a consumer who compounds cannot reopen the finding of guilt — but he may still test the manner of computation of the civil liability, there being a casus omissus in the Act/Code. The appellate order rejecting the Section 127 appeal without a hearing was quashed. Also useful as an agricultural-connection wrong-purpose (Explanation (b)(iv)) example.

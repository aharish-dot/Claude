Harvinder Motors v. BSES Rajdhani Power Ltd.

AIR 2007 Delhi 85; (2006) 135 DLT 198
High Court of Delhi (HC-SB)  |  2006-11-17  |  Bench: S. Ravindra Bhat J (1)  |  IK doc 909057  |  HC-008  |  schema 1.0
Under Section 126 and the DERC Regulations, a case of 'dishonest abstraction of energy' cannot be booked on a missing/tampered seal or broken glass alone unless corroborated by the consumption pattern (no action if consumption exceeds ~75% of assessed), and the show-cause notice must precisely disclose the grounds relied on — failing which the assessment is set aside for breach of natural justice.

Facts & profile

On inspection (29/08/2006) BSES found connected load 64.5 KW against 44 KW sanctioned, with tampered plastic/meter seals and scratches on the CT box; it issued a same-day show-cause notice and a bill of Rs 26,61,507 for DAE. The consumer challenged by writ, arguing seal irregularity alone could not prove DAE and that the consumption-pattern basis (alleged two-shift use) was never disclosed to him.

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126 (incl. 126(3),(5),(6)), DERC Regulations 2002, Reg. 25 & 26

s.126(3)ratiopre-2007 procedure-natural-justice natural justice
An assessment for unauthorised use / DAE must be preceded by inspection and a reasonable hearing; the licensee is bound by the principles of natural justice (Section 126(3) read with Reg. 25(viii)).
“The licensee is obliged to follow principles of natural justice, and grant reasonable hearing”— para 11
s.126ratiopre-2007 precondition-corroboration burden of proof natural justice
A suspicion of unauthorised use / DAE must be corroborated by the consumer's consumption pattern; that corroboration is a precondition to the exercise of assessment jurisdiction, and where consumption exceeds ~75% of the assessed figure no action can be taken. A missing seal or broken glass alone will not do.
“The object of obliging the assessing authority to ensure that consumption pattern, corroborates the suspicion of unauthorized use/ DAE, is a precondition for exercise of jurisdiction; if the consumption is more than 75% of the load, no action can be taken.”— para 12
s.126(3)ratiopre-2007 notice-specificity natural justice
The show-cause notice must precisely and unambiguously state the allegations on which action is proposed; a notice that keeps the real basis (here, the consumption-pattern / two-shift-use finding) from the consumer fails the standard of fairness.
“Notice is not a ritualistic formality; the person requiring to show cause has to be precisely informed, may be briefly, the allegations upon which action is proposed.”— para 14

  • DERC (Performance Standards Metering and Billing) Regulations, 2002, Reg. 25 (booking a case for pilferage/DAE) — implements s.126 procedure (made under Electricity Act, 2003)
  • DERC Regulations, 2002, Reg. 26 (personal hearing; consumption-pattern threshold) — implements s.126 procedure (made under Electricity Act, 2003)

Ratio & what was decided

natural justice HC-SB (Delhi)applies
In a Section 126 unauthorised-use / DAE proceeding the show-cause notice must precisely disclose the grounds — including the consumption-pattern basis relied on — and the licensee must grant a real hearing; a demand founded on seal/glass irregularities without that disclosure or corroboration is set aside and remitted.
burden of proof HC-SB (Delhi)settles
Corroboration of suspected unauthorised use / DAE by the consumer's consumption pattern (Reg. 26(ii)) is a precondition to the exercise of the assessing officer's jurisdiction; if consumption exceeds the prescribed threshold, no case can be booked.

Significance

A frequently-cited (59×) Delhi single-judge authority on the procedural / natural-justice safeguards in Section 126 unauthorised-use (DAE) assessments: seal or glass irregularities alone cannot found a demand without consumption-pattern corroboration, and the show-cause notice must spell out the grounds. Decided on the pre-2007 Section 126 text (1.5× multiplier; 3/6-month presumption).

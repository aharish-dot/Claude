Facts & profile

On inspection the appellants' premises showed direct theft with a tampered meter; the Board complained to the police, an FIR was registered, the police filed a charge-sheet and the Special Judge took cognizance. The accused sought quashing, arguing that under (unamended) Section 151 only a written complaint by an authorised officer could found cognizance, so the police-report route was incompetent (and the 2007 proviso allowing a police report was prospective only).

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.135, s.151 (cognizance), s.153, s.154, CrPC Chapter XII / s.154 / s.173 / s.195

s.135ratiopost-2007 s135-is-cognizable-crpc-applies §135 mens rea jurisdiction (§145/§154)
Theft of electricity under Section 135 (punishable up to three years) is a cognizable offence; the CrPC therefore applies and it is the duty of the police to register an FIR and investigate.
“if the offence under the Code is cognizable, provisions of Chapter XII containing Section 154 Cr.P.C. and onward would become applicable and it would be the duty of the police to register the FIR and investigate into the same.”— para 23
s.151ratiopost-2007 s151-does-not-exclude-police-fir jurisdiction (§145/§154) §135 mens rea
Section 151 enables authorised persons to file a written complaint before the Court (on which cognizance may be taken straightway), but it does not exclude the other avenue of a police FIR/investigation; the Electricity Act is not a complete machinery for how offences are dealt with, so the CrPC governs.
“When such a complaint is filed, the Court would be competent to take cognizance straightway. However, that would not mean that other avenues for investigation into the offence which are available would be excluded.”— para 23
s.151ratiopost-2007 s151-proviso-clarificatory-retrospective jurisdiction (§145/§154)
The amendment (proviso) to Section 151 permitting cognizance on a police report is clarificatory in nature and applies to pending matters (retrospective); notwithstanding Section 151, an FIR could be filed with the police even under the unamended provision.
“conclusively holding that amendment to Section 151 is clarificatory in nature and further that notwithstanding the provisions of Section 151 of the Act, a FIR could be filed with the police”— para 17
s.135 / s.151ratiopost-2007 resolves-hc-split jurisdiction (§145/§154)
The contrary High Court view (complaint-only route) does not lay down correct law; the police-FIR-competent view is approved — resolving the split.
“do not lay down correct law and the view taken by the High Court of Delhi in Abhay Tyagi v. State NCT of Delhi Anr. and Asish Kumar Jain vs. State of Jharkhand (2010) CriLJ 271 is hereby approved.”— para 25

Ratio & what was decided

§135 mens rea SC-bindingsettles-split
Theft of electricity under Section 135 is a cognizable offence governed by the CrPC; the police may register an FIR, investigate and file a Section 173 report on which the Court takes cognizance, and the Section 151 complaint route does not exclude this — the 2007 proviso to Section 151 being clarificatory and retrospective.

Significance

The Supreme Court authority on how a Section 135 prosecution is validly instituted: theft of electricity is a cognizable offence, so the police may register an FIR and file a charge-sheet under the CrPC, and Section 151's complaint route is an additional — not exclusive — avenue; the 2007 proviso to Section 151 (cognizance on a police report) is clarificatory and retrospective. It resolves a High Court split (disapproving the Kerala/Calcutta/Madras 'complaint-only' view, approving the Delhi/Jharkhand view). This anchors the criminal-procedure dimension of Section 135, which the civil-heavy corpus otherwise lacked.

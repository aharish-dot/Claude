M/s Shyam Lal Iron & Steel Co. v. Jharkhand State Electricity Board

L.P.A. No. 59 of 2013 (Jharkhand HC, DB) (2013)
High Court of Jharkhand (HC-DB)  |  2013-04-26  |  Bench: Prakash Tatia CJ, P.P. Bhatt J (2)  |  IK doc 158534755  |  HC-019  |  schema 1.0
For a case treated as theft of electricity (not suspected theft or mere unauthorised use), the Section 126 / Clause 15.7 provisional-assessment-plus-hearing-plus-appeal machinery does not apply — the procedure is Section 135(1A) read with Clause 15.8(i)-(ix), the assessment is examinable only by the Special Court under Section 154(5)/(6), and mislabelling the theft bill as a 'Section 126 provisional assessment' does not convert the proceeding into one under Section 126.

Facts & profile

After the consumer itself reported that miscreants had broken the meter-room seal, an inspection found the LV box M-seal interfered with (accessible secondary terminals); the Board treated it as theft, lodged an FIR, seized the meter and issued a bill labelled a 'Section 126 provisional assessment' (Rs 48.39 lakh). The consumer's writ was disposed of on a 50% deposit; it appealed by LPA, arguing the Clause 15.8 / Section 126 procedure (provisional order, show-cause, 75%-consumption drop) had not been followed.

Disposition: LPA dismissed; the bill was a theft assessment under Section 135(1A)/Clause 15.8(i)-(ix), the mislabel as 'Section 126' was a mistaken belief that did not convert it, and the consumer's remedy against the final assessment lay before the Special Court under Section 154(5)/(6).

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126, s.127, s.135(1A), s.152, s.153, s.154(5)/(6)

s.126 / s.135ratiopost-2007 different-fields-and-procedures §126 vs §135 §135 mens rea
Sections 126 and 135 sit in different Parts (XII vs XIV), cover different fields and prescribe different procedures; theft involves mens rea, which is not necessary for unauthorised use under Section 126.
“different procedures have been prescribed not only by Regulations of 2010 but the Act itself under Section 126 in chapter (xii) and another under Section 135 in chapter (xiv) of the Act.”— para 25
s.135 / Reg. 15.8ratiopost-2007 corroboration-for-suspected-theft burden of proof natural justice
A missing/tampered seal or broken glass window is not, by itself, evidence of theft unless corroborated by the consumer's consumption pattern and other available evidence — this is the safeguard for suspected-theft cases (Clause 15.8(x)-(xv)).
“mere missing of the seal of the meter or tampered or breakage of glass window shall themselves will not be treated to be evidence for theft of electricity unless these facts are corroborated by consumption pattern of the consumer and such other evidence as may be available.”— para 23
Reg. 15.8ratiopost-2007 suspected-vs-clear-theft-procedure §126 vs §135 provisional/final
The suspected-theft safeguards in Clause 15.8(x)-(xv) (continued supply, provisional assessment, show-cause, 75%-consumption drop) have no application to a clear theft case, which is governed by Clause 15.8(i)-(ix).
“We are of the view that procedure as given in sub-clauses (x) to (xv) of Clause 15.8 have no application to the cases of electricity theft.”— para 30
s.126ratiopost-2007 mislabel-does-not-convert §126 vs §135 provisional/final
Labelling a theft demand as a 'Section 126 provisional assessment' does not convert a theft proceeding into a Section 126 assessment where, on the date of inspection, it was treated as theft (FIR lodged) and not even as suspected theft.
“because of sending the communication dated 24th January, 2013, annexing calculation sheet which is calculation of the demand against the person who is found to be indulged in theft will not make any difference nor it will convert the proceeding into one under section 126 of the Act”— para 29
s.127ratiopost-2007 appeal-127-confined-to-126 §127 appeal jurisdiction (§145/§154)
The Section 127 appeal lies only against a Section 126 order, not against a Section 135(1A)/Clause 15.8 theft assessment; the latter is examinable by the Special Court under Section 154(5)/(6).
“under Section 127 which is statutory provision, right to appeal has been provided only against the order passed under Section 126 and not against the order passed under provision of Section 135 (1A) or under clause 15.8 of the Regulations 2010.”— para 27
s.154(5)/(6)ratiopost-2007 forum-special-court jurisdiction (§145/§154)
The remedy against a final theft assessment is before the Special Court under Section 154(5)/(6), which determines the civil liability; the writ court will not interfere.
“against this order, statutory remedy is available to the appellant under section 154 (5) and (6) of the Act.”— para 35

Ratio & what was decided

§126 vs §135 HC-DB (Jharkhand)settles-explains
Where a case is treated as theft (FIR lodged, not suspected theft or mere unauthorised use), the Section 126 / Clause 15.7 provisional-assessment machinery and the suspected-theft safeguards of Clause 15.8(x)-(xv) do not apply; the procedure is Section 135(1A)/Clause 15.8(i)-(ix), and mislabelling the theft bill as a Section 126 assessment does not convert the proceeding.
§127 appeal HC-DB (Jharkhand)settles
A Section 127 appeal lies only against a Section 126 order; a Section 135(1A)/theft assessment is not appealable under Section 127 and is examinable only by the Special Court under Section 154(5)/(6).

Significance

A reportable Jharkhand Division-Bench authority mapping the three tracks — unauthorised use (Section 126/Clause 15.7), clear theft (Section 135(1A)/Clause 15.8(i)-(ix)) and suspected theft (Clause 15.8(x)-(xv)) — and holding that a theft assessment cannot be recast as a Section 126 assessment by mislabelling, is not appealable under Section 127, and is examinable only by the Special Court under Section 154(5)/(6). Confirms the corroboration safeguard (seal/glass alone insufficient) applies to suspected-theft cases. Applies Seetaram Rice Mill; the appellant relied on Harvinder Motors (HC-008) and Hasi Mazumdar (HC-021).

Facts & profile

An industrial (paper mill) consumer's billing meter was found burnt to ash on inspection; the checking team recommended action under Section 138(d), but a Section 126 provisional and final theft assessment was raised (relying on readings of three meters installed OUTSIDE the premises which, until the meter burnt, matched the billing meter). No oral hearing was given, and the final assessment recorded no reasoning establishing theft. The appellate authority (Commissioner) upheld it in a non-speaking order.

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126, s.127, s.135, s.138(d)

s.126 / Reg. 8.1ratiopost-2007 must-establish-theft-first §126 assessment burden of proof
The assessing officer has no jurisdiction to make a theft assessment unless he first establishes a case of theft against the person (para 8.1(b)(iv)); merely issuing a provisional assessment order does not establish theft.
“The Assessing Officer, I have no hesitation to hold, had no jurisdiction at all to proceed to make assessment unless he establishes a case of theft against the person concerned.”— para 183
s.126ratiopost-2007 theft-not-on-presumption burden of proof
Theft of electrical energy cannot be made out on mere presumption; it has to be established as required by para 8.1(b)(iv) of the Code.
“a case of theft of electrical energy cannot be made out on mere presumption. It has to be established as per para 8.1 (b)(iv).”— para 195
s.126(1)ratiopost-2007 best-judgment-rational-unbiased §126 assessment
A best-judgment assessment under Section 126 must be arrived at without bias and on a rational basis; the officer cannot take a 'short cut' by recording an evasive conclusion in the provisional assessment without discussing how the inference of unauthorised use/theft was reached.
“The Assessing Officer thus in my view, opted for a short cut by issuing a provisional assessment order without discussing anything to show how he could arrive at the inference that petitioner is guilty of unauthorized use of electricity or theft and in an evasive manner simply recorded his conclusion and proceeded to make provisional assessment.”— para 181
s.126(3) / Reg. 8.1ratiopost-2007 oral-hearing-required natural justice
The law requires an opportunity of oral hearing to the consumer before final assessment; none was given here (no date was communicated for the consumer to answer the charges in person).
“The law also requires an opportunity of oral hearing to the consumer but in the case in hand, admittedly no such opportunity was given to the petitioner”— para 183
s.126 / s.135explanatorypost-2007 s126-vs-s135-positive-act §126 vs §135 §135 mens rea
Section 126 assessment may be made even on inspection of records where a person is found in unauthorised use, without attracting Section 135, because Section 135 contemplates a positive act interfering with the recording of electricity supplied.
“for the purpose of Section 126, if Assessing Officer even after inspection of record maintained by a person comes to the conclusion that such person is indulged in unauthorized use of electricity, he can proceed to make assessment but then Section 135 would not be attracted. The reason being that it contemplates a positive act”— para 72
s.138(d)ratiopost-2007 no-s126-assessment-for-138(d) §126 assessment
For a contravention of Section 138(d), an assessment under Section 126 (or para 8.1 of the Code) is not permissible.
“for contravention of Section 138(d) , assessment under Section 126 or in para 8.1 is not permissible.”— para 187

Ratio & what was decided

§126 assessment HC-SB (Allahabad)settles
An assessing officer cannot make a theft assessment without first establishing a case of theft (para 8.1(b)(iv)); the best-judgment assessment must be reasoned, unbiased and rational, an oral hearing must be given, and theft cannot be inferred on mere presumption or on readings of meters not installed at the consumer's premises absent a specific enabling provision.
§127 appeal HC-SB (Allahabad)affirms
The Section 127 appellate authority must apply its own mind, read the relevant provisions and pass a reasoned, speaking order; a shallow, non-speaking affirmance cannot stand.

Significance

A detailed, reportable Allahabad authority (cited 7×, and pre-dating Seetaram Rice Mill) on the discipline of a Section 126 theft assessment: the assessing officer has no jurisdiction to assess for theft unless theft is first established (para 8.1(b)(iv)), the best-judgment assessment must be unbiased, rational and reasoned (Haji Lal Mohd. Biri Works), an oral hearing is mandatory, theft cannot rest on presumption, and reliance on meters not installed at the consumer's premises needs a specific enabling provision (subject to the Lex-non-cogit exception). Also holds that a Section 138(d) contravention does not permit a Section 126 assessment, and the Section 127 appellate order must be a speaking one.

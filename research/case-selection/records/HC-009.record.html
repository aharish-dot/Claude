Facts & profile

On inspection the meter was found dead-stopped with a wire soldered in the counter circuit after the counter lock had been broken; the Nigam treated it as established theft, made an assessment of Rs 11,66,650 and issued a Section 135 read with Section 152 notice offering compounding. The consumer challenged the assessment by writ, contending a show-cause notice was mandatory and that the amount could only be assessed by the Special Court under Section 154(5) (or under Section 126 after notice).

Disposition: Writ petitions dismissed; the Section 154(5) formula used by the Nigam was upheld, no hearing was required as the theft was writ large, and the consumer was left to accept the compounding notice or face trial before the Special Court.

Issues framed

  1. Whether in case of theft, the consumer requires an opportunity of hearing or opportunity of hearing is only given in the case of suspected theft?
  2. Whether the assessment under Section 135 of the Act has to be carried out in terms of Section 126 of the Act or in terms of Section 154 (5) of the Act as has been done by the respondents in this case?

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126, s.135, s.152, s.154(5)

s.126 / s.135ratiopost-2007 different-fields §126 vs §135
Sections 126 and 135 operate in different fields: Section 126 is invoked for over-consumption / unauthorised use of electricity, whereas Section 135 is invoked for theft of electricity.
“Sections 126 and 135 of the Act operate in different fields. Section 126 is invoked in case of over-consumption of electricity whereas Section 135 of the Act is invoked in case of theft of electricity.”— para (analysis)
s.126 / s.154(5)ratiopost-2007 assessment-measure-for-theft §126 vs §135 jurisdiction (§145/§154)
Section 126 cannot be applied to assess the amount to be charged for theft of electricity; the civil liability for theft is measured under the Section 154(5) formula (twice the tariff for the twelve months preceding detection, or the exact period of theft if determined, whichever is less), subject to final adjudication by the Special Court.
“provisions of Section 126 of the Act cannot be applied for the purpose of assessment of amount, which is to be charged for theft of electricity.”— para (analysis)
s.135ratiopost-2007 hearing-suspected-vs-writ-large natural justice §135 mens rea
A show-cause notice / opportunity of hearing is required only where theft is suspected (broken/tampered seals, no evidence at site — per Circular 54/2007); where the theft is 'writ large' (here a dead-stop meter with a soldered wire after breaking the counter lock), no opportunity of hearing need be given before proceeding.
“in the case of suspected theft, opportunity of hearing has to be given but for the theft, which is writ large, as admitted by learned counsel for the respondents, no opportunity is required to be given.”— para (analysis)
s.152ratiopost-2007 compounding-scope §152 compounding
The remedy of compounding under Section 152 is available in both cases — established theft and suspected theft; accepting the compounding notice avoids trial before the Special Court, refusing it exposes the consumer to punishment and fine by that Court.
“So far as Section 152 of the Act in respect of compounding of the offence is concerned, it is applied in both the cases, where there is theft and is suspected theft.”— para (analysis)

Ratio & what was decided

§126 vs §135 HC-SB (Punjab & Haryana)applies
Sections 126 and 135 operate in different and distinct fields; theft under Section 135 cannot be assessed under Section 126, and its civil liability is measured under the Section 154(5) formula, subject to final adjudication by the Special Court.
natural justice HC-SB (Punjab & Haryana)settles
A pre-assessment hearing in a theft case is required only where theft is merely suspected; where theft is 'writ large' on the checking report, no opportunity of hearing need be afforded before the licensee proceeds.
§152 compounding HC-SB (Punjab & Haryana)affirms
Compounding under Section 152 is available both for established and for suspected theft; it is offered before the consumer is proceeded against before the Special Court.

Significance

A Punjab & Haryana single-judge authority applying the Seetaram Rice Mill 126-vs-135 distinction to hold three practical points: (1) theft is assessed under the Section 154(5) formula, not Section 126; (2) a hearing is due only for suspected theft, not theft 'writ large'; and (3) Section 152 compounding is available in both situations. Useful on the interface between the Section 135/154(5) theft channel and the Section 126 assessment channel, and on the 'writ large' exception to natural justice.

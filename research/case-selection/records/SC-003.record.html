West Bengal State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. (WBSEDCL) v. M/s Orion Metal Pvt. Ltd.

(2020) 18 SCC 588; Civil Appeal No. 6547 of 2019
Supreme Court of India (SC)  |  2019-08-21  |  Bench: R. Banumathi J, R. Subhash Reddy J (2)  |  IK doc 95828096  |  SC-003  |  schema 1.0
The Section 126(1) assessment power and the Special Court's Section 154(5) civil-liability power are not parallel/mutually exclusive: theft under Section 135 falls within 'unauthorised use', so a Section 126 assessment may be made even in theft cases, and where the unauthorised use amounts to theft both a Section 126 assessment and a Section 135 complaint (with Section 154(5) civil liability) may proceed — the Section 126 assessment needing only civil proof, not proof beyond reasonable doubt.

Facts & profile

On inspection of a bulk HT consumer (1450 KVA), the team found abnormal current at the PT secondary wires and foreign material inside the TTB, treated it as theft by meter-tampering, seized the meter, made a Section 126(1) provisional assessment of ~Rs 13.41 crore for un-metered consumption, and lodged a criminal complaint (charge-sheet filed). The consumer's writ succeeded before a Division Bench, which held that Section 126 could be invoked only where restoration of supply was sought and that otherwise civil liability lay only with the Special Court under Section 154(5). WBSEDCL appealed.

Issues framed

Interpretation of the Electricity Act

Provisions construed: s.126, s.126(1), s.135, s.135(1A), s.154(5)/(6)

s.126 / s.135ratiopost-2007 theft-is-a-subset-of-unauthorised-use §126 vs §135
All instances of unauthorised use may not amount to theft, but theft under Section 135 falls within the definition of 'unauthorised use of electricity'; hence use through a tampered meter (Section 126(6)(b)(iii)) is unauthorised use assessable under Section 126.
“All instances of unauthorized use of energy may not amount to theft of electricity within the meaning of Section 135 of the Act, but at the same time, the theft of electricity which is covered by Section 135 of the Act, will fall within the definition of unauthorized use of electricity.”— para 12
s.126(1)ratiopost-2007 s126-available-in-theft §126 assessment §126 vs §135
The authorities are empowered to make a provisional and final assessment under Section 126(1) even in cases where electricity is unauthorisedly used by way of theft.
“the authorities under the Act are empowered to make a provisional and final assessment by invoking power under Section 126(1) of the Act, even in cases where electricity is unauthorisedly used by way of theft.”— para 12
s.126 / s.154(5)ratiopost-2007 not-parallel-both-may-operate jurisdiction (§145/§154) §126 vs §135
The Section 126(1) assessment power and the Section 154(5) Special Court power to determine civil liability are not parallel to each other; the High Court erred in holding that both proceedings cannot operate parallelly.
“The power conferred on authorities for making assessment under Section 126(1) of the Act and power to determine civil liability under Section 154(5) of the Act, cannot be said to be parallel to each other. In this regard, we are of the view that the High Court has committed an error in recording a finding, that both proceedings cannot operate parallelly.”— para 14
s.126 / s.135 / s.154(5)ratiopost-2007 two-scenarios-mapping §126 vs §135 jurisdiction (§145/§154)
Where there is no theft (only unauthorised use), no Section 135 complaint lies and the loss is assessed under Section 126(1); where the unauthorised use amounts to theft, a Section 126(1) assessment AND a Section 135 complaint may both proceed, with the Special Court determining civil liability under Section 154(5) and any excess deposit refunded.
“In cases where allegation is of unauthorized use of energy amounting to theft, in such cases, apart from assessing the proceedings under Section 126(1) of the Act, a complaint also can be lodged alleging theft of energy as defined under Section 135(1) of the Act. In such cases, the Special Court is empowered to determine civil liability under Section 154(5) of the Act.”— para 14
s.126 / s.135ratiopost-2007 proof-standard-civil-vs-criminal burden of proof §135 mens rea
To prove theft in a criminal proceeding, the case must be proved beyond reasonable doubt with mens rea established; such strict proof is not necessary for assessing liability under Section 126(1).
“to prove the guilt of the accused in a criminal proceeding, authorities have to prove the case beyond reasonable doubt and the element of mens rea is also to be established. On the other hand, such a strict proof is not necessary for assessing the liability under Section 126(1) of the Act.”— para 14
s.154(5)ratiopost-2007 s154-does-not-oust-s126 jurisdiction (§145/§154)
Merely because the Special Court is empowered to determine civil liability under Section 154(5) where a complaint is lodged, it cannot be said that the authorities have no power to make a Section 126 assessment.
“Merely because the Special Court is empowered to determine civil liability under Section 154(5) of the Act, in cases where a complaint is lodged, it cannot be said that there is no power conferred on authorities to make provisional assessment/final assessment under Section 126 of the Act.”— para 12

Ratio & what was decided

§126 vs §135 SC-bindingsettles
Theft under Section 135 is a subset of 'unauthorised use', so a Section 126 assessment may be made even in theft cases; the Section 126(1) assessment and the Section 154(5) Special Court determination are not parallel/mutually exclusive and may both proceed, the Section 126 assessment requiring only civil proof (preponderance), not proof beyond reasonable doubt.

Significance

The leading Supreme Court authority (after Seetaram) on the interplay of Section 126, Section 135 and Section 154(5): it settles that theft is a subset of unauthorised use, that a Section 126 assessment may be made even in theft cases, and that the Section 126 assessment and the Special Court's Section 154(5) civil-liability determination are not parallel/mutually exclusive — both may run, with the Section 126 assessment requiring only civil proof. Applies Seetaram Rice Mill (SC-002). Widely relied on across the corpus (HC-017, HC-022, HC-024).

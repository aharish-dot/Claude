ORDER
1. The present IA has been filed by Trimurti Concast Pvt. Ltd. the 
Applicant/Corporate Debtor, through its Director Sh. Satish Chand Goel, 
under Section 60(5) of the Insolvency And Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (I & 
B Code, 2016), read with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 on account 
of the contravention in terms of the provisions of Section 74(3) with 
regard to Resolution Plan approved by this Tribunal vide order dated 
22.03.2022. Mr. Satish Chand Goel through whom this petition is filed 
by the Corporate Debtor, is newly appointed Director after its 
management was taken over by the Successful Resolution Applicant 
(SRA) i.e. Shree Shailja Iron and Steel Ltd. as per order dated 
22.03.2022 by this Tribunal and suspended Ex-Directors of the 
Corporate Debtor during pre-Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process 
(CIRP) had been discharged from their duties towards the Applicant i.e. 
Corporate Debtor. The Applicant herein is seeking direction upon 
Respondent i.e. Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited (PVVNL) 
(Power Supplying Company) to grant new electricity connection and 
supply of electric energy at the office and plant of the Applicant.
2. This Tribunal vide its order dated 24.12.2019 admitted the 
Section 7 Application and initiated Corporate Insolvency Resolution 
Process against the Corporate Debtor, namely Trimurti Concast Private 
Limited. Subsequently this tribunal Vide order dated 22.03.2022 
approved the Resolution Plan submitted by the SRA. for the revival of 
the Applicant/Corporate Debtor.

support of this contention, the respondent has also placed reliance on a 
judgment of the Hon'ble Allahabad High Court delivered vide order

dated 12.01.2023 in WR-C No. 26355 of 2022 in the case of Narendra 
Singh Panwar v. Pashimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited The 
operative part of this judgment is already discussed in para 4(vi) of this 
order. We have perused this judgment and find that reliance of the 
respondent on this judgment is misplaced. In this case, recovery notice 
for payment of outstanding dues of electricity was issued to Ex-
Directors of the Corporate Debtor under consideration in this application 
and they challenged this recovery notice taking the plea that a 
Resolution Plan in the case of the Corporate Debtor has already been 
passed, and therefore, such recovery cannot be made as all such claims 
are now extinguished. On this writ petition filed by the Ex-Directors of 
the Corporate Debtor, it has been held by the Hon'ble Allahabad High 
Court that the object of the code is not to allow personal guarantors 
such as Directors who are in management of the companies to escape 
from an independent and co-existent liability to pay off the entire 
outstanding debt and therefore, it is further held that approval of a 
Resolution Plan does not ipso facto absolve the surety/guarantor of his 
or her liability, which arises out of an independent contract of 
guarantee. Having decided above, the Hon'ble High Court finally held 
that the challenge to the demand notice for dues of electricity, issued 
jointly in the name of the Directors of the Corporate Debtor, the 
defaulter company which went into insolvency cannot be sustained on 
the ground that in view of the acceptance of the Resolution Plan under 
Section 31 of the Code, all liabilities of Directors who may be the 
guarantor, stood automatically discharged/extinguished.
7. From the above decision, it is very clear that the Hon'ble 
Allahabad High Court has only held about the enforceability of liabilities 
of the Directors of Corporate Debtor who were running the said 
Corporate Debtor during Pre-CIRP period and their liability is in terms 
of surety/guarantee provided by them in terms of the agreement. 
However, Corporate Debtor being an independent entity would not be 
liable to pay any liability of Pre-CIRP period after a Resolution Plan has 
been approved under Section 31 of the I&B Code. In this regard, the 
reliance placed by the applicant on the decision of the Hon'ble Supreme 
Court of India in Civil Appeal No. 8129/2019 dated 13th April, 2021 in 
the case of Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited through the 
Authorized Signatory v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company 
Limited through the Director is relevant. The relevant part of the 
decision of the Hon'ble Apex Court in this regard is as under:—
“95(i) That once a resolution plan is duly approved by the

in the I&B Code with regard to liability for prior offences etc. inserted 
by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Management Act, 2020) w.e.f.

9. In view of the above decision of the of the Hon'ble Apex Court and 
provision of Section 32A as discussed above, now no action can be 
taken against a Corporate Debtor with respect to the property relating 
to offence committed prior to the commencement of the Corporate 
Insolvency Resolution Process of the Corporate Debtor which results 
change in control, by any person including the Central Government, any 
State Government or any Local Authority etc. Therefore, we hold that 
the Respondent Electricity Supplying Company is not legally correct in 
rejecting the application of the Applicant/Corporate Debtor for a new 
electricity connection on account of outstanding dues pertaining to Pre-
CIRP period, hence, the respondent supplying company is directed to 
consider the application of the Applicant/Corporate Debtor for a new 
electricity connection and take immediate steps to provide the 
electricity connections to support the business of the Corporate Debtor 
immediately as a going concern meeting the objective of I&B Code, 
2016. Needless to say that while providing new electricity connection to 
the Corporate Debtor, the provisions of the Electricity Act, 2003 r/w. 
U.P. Electricity Supply Code, 2005 otherwise shall be adhered to except 
recovery of Pre-CIRP dues from the Corporate Debtor.
10. In the result, IA No. 409/2022 is allowed and stands disposed 
off.

Bihar Electricity Regulatory Commission Bihar Electricity Supply 
Code, 2007
Chapter 4. New Service Connection
Rule 4-15.
4.15. No Dues Certificate.—(i) It will be the duty of the seller to find 
out the outstanding electricity dues up to the date of sale, and liable to 
pay the outstanding electricity dues/obtain no dues certificate.
(ii) Before sale of a premise is made, the outstanding dues will be 
cleared or alternatively the agreement/sale deed will specifically 
mention the outstanding dues and the method of its payment. 
“Outstanding dues” means all dues pending on a premise including late 
payment surcharge.
(iii) In the event of non payment of the outstanding dues, the dues 
shall be recoverable as public demand under the Bihar and Orissa 
Public Demand Recovery Act, 1954 as amended from time to time.
(iv) The outstanding dues will be first charge on the assets of the 
defaulting consumer/company, and the licensee shall ensure that this is 
entered in an agreement with new applicant.
(v) The recovery proceedings against the defaulting consumer, and 
where the defaulting consumer is a company, from the Directors of the 
company, shall be ensured. Where a financial institution has auctioned 
the property without consideration to licensees charge on assets, claims 
may be lodged with the concerned financial institution with diligent 
pursuance.
(vi) in case the electricity connection to the said premises was given 
with the consent of house owner, such person shall ensure the payment 
of all arrears/dues of electricity by the tenant before the tenant vacates 
the premises.
However the conditions mentioned under clause 4.15 above shall not 
apply if inconsistant with the provision of any higher court order or an 
order as a consequence to it.

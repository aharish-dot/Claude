# The Electricity Supply Code — A Jurisprudence

## Master Outline & Build Plan

> **What this is.** A single, authored, navigable treatise synthesising the 151-judgment
> corpus (`supply-code/summaries/json/SCJ-001` … `SCJ-151`) into a body of jurisprudence
> organised by *theme → statutory provision → legal principle*, with every proposition pinned
> to the case(s) that establish it. This is the culminating deliverable the project's
> `README.md` describes: *"not just 300 isolated case notes, but a spine … so a reader can ask
> 'what have the courts held about Clause 4.26(e)?' … and get every case, with its holding."*
>
> The machine-generated `jurisprudence/index.json` is the **data spine** (provision- and
> principle-keyed, regenerated by `tools/build_supply_code.py`). This treatise is the
> **authored synthesis** that reads that spine into continuous doctrine. The two are kept
> consistent: every case ID, provision key, and principle tag used here exists in the index.

---

## How this document is built (conventions carried across sessions)

1. **Multi-file, one Part per file.** Each Part below is authored as its own file
   (`NN-part-slug.md`). This outline (`00-OUTLINE.md`) is the index and the **progress
   tracker**. Completing a Part = writing its file + ticking its box here + committing.
2. **Citation discipline.** Every doctrinal statement carries its case ID(s) inline — e.g.
   *(SCJ-147; SCJ-042; SCJ-069)*. Where a paragraph pin exists in the source record, cite it
   as *SCJ-147 ¶53*. External authorities are named with their reporter citation and their
   **treatment** (followed / distinguished / applied) exactly as the underlying record tags it.
3. **Provision keys** use the index's convention: `UP-2005::6.8`, `EA2003::126`,
   `WB-2004::5.2`, etc. State Codes are never merged into Act sections (the Code-clause /
   Act-section conflation is itself a documented data-quality point — see Part I §4).
4. **No new law.** The treatise only synthesises what the 151 records hold. Where the corpus
   leaves a question open, it goes to Part X (Register of Open Questions), not papered over.
5. **Neutrality on unresolved tensions.** Where corpus cases pull in different directions
   (e.g. "s.126 covers use *not amounting to theft*" vs. the U.P. line routing theft
   assessments through s.126), the treatise states the tension and how the cases reconcile it,
   rather than picking a side the corpus has not.

## THE OUTLINE

### PART I — Framework, Sources & Method  · `01-framework.md`
- **§1. The statutory architecture.** Electricity Act 2003 as the parent code; the shift from
  the 1910 Act and 1948 Supply Act (history recounted in SCJ-147, SCJ-151, SCJ-137); State
  Electricity Regulatory Commissions and their rule-making power (ss.50, 181).
- **§2. What a "Supply Code" is and is not.** State Codes (`UP-2005`, `WB-2004`, `JH-2005`,
  `UP-2002`) as delegated legislation under s.50; the confines of that power. Anchor: **Thomas
  Joseph (SCJ-147)** — a Code cannot supplant the Act; **Anis Ahmad (SCJ-139)** — a Code "does
  not relate to" s.126 assessment.
- **§3. The Act–Code hierarchy in practice.** Where a Code clause conflicts with the Act, the
  Act prevails (SCJ-059 `EA2003::126`; SCJ-147 declaring Reg. 153(15) ultra vires). "Clause
  heading not a limit on substantive scope" (principle; SCJ-041, SCJ-123).
- **§4. Precision rules & data-quality conventions.** Code-clause vs Act-section conflation
  (e.g. "Clause 56(2)" of a Code vs s.56(2) of the Act — SCJ-034 and the `EA2003::56(2)` /
  `UP-2005` split); multiple State Codes not merged; how the index keys provisions.
- **§5. Scope of this treatise.** The three tiers of the corpus: (a) core Supply-Code cases;
  (b) Act-architecture cases; (c) peripheral/off-topic cases held for the master citation
  ledger. How each tier is treated here.

### PART II — Assessment, Unauthorised Use & Theft  · `02-assessment-theft.md`
*The corpus's centre of gravity — ~30 cases.*
- **§1. Section 126 as the exclusive assessment mechanism.** s.135 creates only the offence;
  it houses no assessment machinery (its 1A-proviso presupposes a s.126 "assessed amount").
  Anchor: **Trikoot Iron (SCJ-069)**; **Anis Ahmad (SCJ-139)**.
- **§2. s.126 vs s.135 — distinct fields.** Civil/preventive vs criminal; mens rea irrelevant
  to the civil assessment. Principle `126-vs-135-distinct-fields` (SCJ-051, SCJ-061, SCJ-121);
  the reconciliation of "not amounting to theft" language with the U.P. theft-assessment line.
- **§3. "Unauthorised use of electricity" — the wide, purposive meaning.** Explanation (b) is
  not exhaustive; excess/additional load = unauthorised use even without tariff change or
  revenue loss. Anchor: **Thomas Joseph (SCJ-147)** applying **Seetaram Rice Mill**; SCJ-042,
  SCJ-111, SCJ-121.
- **§4. Tariff = fixed + energy charges.** s.45(3)(a); assessment at twice the full tariff
  (SCJ-147). Rate/period under s.126(5)-(6); the 2007 amendment (1.5× → 2×).
- **§5. Two procedures, one assessment: Clause 6.8 vs Clause 8.1–8.3.** Specific-over-general
  (Mohit Paper Mills line); theft ⇒ Clauses 8.1–8.3, other unauthorised use ⇒ Clause 6.8; both
  remain s.126 assessments (SCJ-069, SCJ-060, SCJ-121, SCJ-070, SCJ-059).
- **§6. Natural justice in assessment.** Provisional order → mandatory notice (15 working
  days: Clause 8.1(b)(iii)) → hearing → **reasoned** final order under s.126(3). Principle
  `opportunity-before-final-assessment` (SCJ-014, SCJ-022, SCJ-028, SCJ-117, SCJ-126); "final
  notice" that skips the hearing is a nullity (SCJ-117).
- **§7. Malice in law / fabricated checking reports.** When the writ court *will* intervene
  despite the alternative remedy. Anchor: **Vimla Tiwari (SCJ-126)**.
- **§8. Meter testing — Clause 5.6 (and 5.6(d)).** Applies to "any meter" not only
  known-defective ones; manufacturer's lab is not "independent"; pre-testing notice (Amrawati
  Devi / Modern Rice Mill line). `UP-2005::5.6` = SCJ-058, SCJ-059, SCJ-064, SCJ-071, SCJ-126;
  `UP-2005::5.6(d)` = SCJ-003, SCJ-114. Interlock with IEA-1910 s.26(6) — fraud/tampering
  falls outside the Electrical-Inspector route (**SCJ-151**).
- **§9. The appeal — Section 127.** Theft-based assessments *are* appealable; appellate power
  co-extensive with assessing officer's; 50% pre-deposit (s.127(2)); only *final* orders
  appealable; s.127(4) finality. Anchor: **Trikoot Iron (SCJ-069)**; SCJ-121; SCJ-069/SCJ-042.
  Structural independence of the Appellate Authority (SCJ-126).
- **§10. Interlock with the criminal trial.** Clause 8.2(X)(g)/(h) — assessment "subject to"
  the Special Court; acquittal ⇒ withdrawal & meter restoration (SCJ-121). s.152 compounding;
  s.153 Special Courts; s.154 civil liability; s.135 arrest safeguards (principle
  `s135-arrest-safeguards-and-compounding-remedy`).
- **§11. Recovery presupposes a final assessment.** No 1958-Act citation without a s.126(3)
  order; the State-wide systemic direction. Anchor: **Vinod Kumar Agarwal (SCJ-145)**; SCJ-022,
  SCJ-028; `UPDR1958::3`.
- **§12. Evidentiary threshold for a theft inference.** Physical/MRI indicators; what suffices
  and what does not (SCJ-051, SCJ-060, SCJ-070); tampered-seal-without-more insufficient (Abdul
  Gani line).

### PART III — Arrears & Transferee Liability  · `03-arrears-transferee.md`
*The second dominant mass — 9 cases on the lead principle alone.*
- **§1. The foundational line.** Isha Marbles → Ahmedabad Electricity/Gujarat Inns → **DVS
  Steels** → **Hanuman Rice Mills** → **K.C. Ninan** — the Supreme Court arc, as the corpus
  receives it (SCJ-099, SCJ-127, and applied throughout).
- **§2. "Arrears run with the premises" — the rule and its true basis.** No charge in general
  law; liability arises only where a *statutory* Code clause or supply condition creates it
  (principle `arrears-run-with-the-premises`, 9 cases: SCJ-031, SCJ-037, SCJ-054, SCJ-098,
  SCJ-099, SCJ-125, SCJ-127, and more).
- **§3. Fresh connection vs transfer of connection.** The dividing line that decides liability
  (Raghunath Paper Mills SCJ-127; the transfer bar in `WESCO`/Orissa and U.P. clauses).
- **§4. Premises-specific bar; apportionment on subdivision.** `arrears-bar-is-premises-
  specific`; `arrears-apportioned-pro-rata-on-subdivision` (SCJ-031, SCJ-007).
- **§5. The transferee contexts.** Auction purchaser; IBC/liquidation (SCJ-098 with Sundaresh
  Bhatt); development-authority / EWS allotment (**Pawan Jha SCJ-125**); private sale.
- **§6. Company & director liability.** Clause 4.3(f)(iv)-(v); first charge on company assets,
  directors only if insufficient; not retrospective. Anchor: **Piyush Kumar (SCJ-129)**.
- **§7. Landlord–tenant liability.** Clause 4.3(f)(vi) co-extensive liability; consent via
  tenancy clause. Anchor: **Beena Dubey (SCJ-124)**.

### PART IV — New Connections, Feeders & Infrastructure  · `04-connections-infrastructure.md`
- **§1. Duty to supply (s.43) & the connection process (Clause 4.x).** Timelines, security.
- **§2. Occupant connection on indemnity bond — Clause 4.4.** 8-case cluster; occupier
  entitled to a connection on an indemnity bond irrespective of ownership dispute (principles
  `clause-4.4-occupant-connection-on-indemnity-bond`, `occupier-entitled-to-connection-on-
  indemnity-bond`).
- **§3. Independent-feeder requirement — Clause 3.4.** Applies to load enhancement, not only
  new connections; "mixed feeder" ≠ "independent feeder" (Clause 2.2(ff)). Anchor: **Sigma
  Castings (SCJ-123)**; `UP-2005::independent-feeder-definition`.
- **§4. Landowner objection to supply lines — Clause 4.5(d) + Works of Licensees Rules 2006.**
  Resolution by the District Magistrate; balancing private right vs public electrification
  (SCJ-104, SCJ-120).
- **§5. Security, bank guarantees & assessment-linked conditions — Clauses 4.49, 4.21(a),
  4.26(e), 4.37.** Stale-assessment-based guarantee quashed (SCJ-123).
- **§6. Supervision charges & self-executed works.** Statutory Code rate supersedes an
  administrative memorandum. Anchor: **Avas Evam Vikas Parishad (SCJ-137)** (`UP-2002::4.45`).
- **§7. Tariff classification.** Profession vs commerce; advocates' chambers = domestic tariff
  (Clause 3.3(e)). Anchor: **Tehsil Bar Association (SCJ-133)**.

### PART V — Billing, Metering & Consumer Grievances  · `05-billing-grievance-forum.md`
- **§1. Billing provisions — Clauses 6.5, 6.14–6.16.** Corrected/supplementary bills; the
  bill-must-be-served line.
- **§2. Limitation on recovery — s.56(2) of the Act.** The two-year bar; what "first became
  due" means; penalty/surcharge vs charges (SCJ-034; the `EA2003::56` / `56(2)` cluster;
  Chandra Mohan Goyal SCJ-128 on retrospective circular application).
- **§3. Grievance redressal — Clause 7.10, s.42(5)-(8).** Forum → Ombudsman route for billing
  grievances (principle `clause-7.10-forum-ombudsman-route-for-billing-grievances`).
- **§4. The Electricity Ombudsman's status.** Neither an Appellate Authority nor a Tribunal;
  ADR-type quasi-judicial body; no finality; consumer-only remedy. Anchor: **UPPCL v.
  Electricity Ombudsman (SCJ-138)**; `ombudsman-remedy-consumer-only`.
- **§5. Consumer-forum exclusion.** A CP-Act complaint against a s.126 assessment or ss.135–140
  action is not maintainable; commercial consumers not "consumers". Anchor: **Anis Ahmad
  (SCJ-139)**.

### PART VI — Remedies, Forum Selection & the Limits of Jurisdiction  · `06-remedies-jurisdiction.md`
- **§1. Alternative remedy & relegation.** The statutory remedy (objection → s.127 appeal /
  Forum → Ombudsman) must ordinarily be exhausted before the writ; when it may be bypassed
  (jurisdictional error, natural-justice breach, vires). Principles `relegate-to-statutory-
  remedy`, `alternative-remedy-relegation`, `meter-dispute-must-exhaust-clause-5.6-before-
  writ` (SCJ-143, SCJ-063, Harbanslal Sahnia exceptions).
- **§2. The Code forum is not for tortious damages.** `code-forum-not-for-tortious-damages`
  (SCJ-015, SCJ-019, SCJ-025): CGRF/Ombudsman and the s.145 bar cover *consumer* disputes; a
  non-consumer's tort/electrocution damages claim goes to the Permanent Lok Adalat as a
  public-utility-service dispute. Cross-refers Part VIII §2 (hazardous-activity strict duty,
  SCJ-019, SCJ-025).
- **§3. Disconnection is not a debt-collection / civil-dispute weapon.** Not to be used to
  resolve landlord-tenant or title disputes; a connection confers no title
  (`disconnection-not-for-landlord-tenant-dispute`: SCJ-016, SCJ-018, SCJ-020).
- **§4. Civil-court bar — s.145; jurisdiction not by consent.** (SCJ-139; `OFFTOPIC::
  jurisdiction-not-by-consent` SCJ-…). Delay & laches in the writ (SCJ-…).

### PART VII — Regulatory Architecture (Act-level)  · `07-regulatory-architecture.md`
- **§1. The Supply Code as delegated legislation — vires.** Full ultra-vires doctrine
  (Sukhdev Singh, Global Energy, P. Krishnamurthy line) as deployed in **Thomas Joseph
  (SCJ-147)** to strike Reg. 153(15). The delegation cannot create substantive rights/
  exemptions absent in the parent Act.
- **§2. SERC composition — the judicial-member requirement.** s.84(2) enabling, not mandatory,
  for a judge-Chairperson, but a legally-qualified Member is mandatory for adjudication.
  Anchor: **State of Gujarat v. Utility Users (SCJ-130)**.
- **§3. Franchisee as agent of the licensee.** No direct ERC oversight of a franchisee; s.128
  investigation reaches only the licensee. Anchor: **Torrent Power (SCJ-136)**.
- **§4. APTEL / CERC jurisdiction; tariff & open access.** APTEL over CERC regulations;
  tariff determination; cross-subsidy surcharge; SEZ deemed licensee (SCJ-106 and the
  `OFFTOPIC::aptel-…`, `::cross-subsidy-…`, `::rerc-open-access-…`, `::s62-6-interest-…`
  cluster — compact treatment, flagged as Act-architecture not Supply-Code retail).

### PART VIII — Cross-cutting Constitutional & General-Law Principles  · `08-cross-cutting-principles.md`
- **§1. Right to electricity under Article 21.** `right-to-electricity-art21` (2 cases).
- **§2. Hazardous activity & strict duty of the licensee.** `hazardous-activity-strict-duty`
  (2 cases); the safety rationale (also SCJ-137 supervision-charge reasoning).
- **§3. Natural justice — the spectrum.** No rigid rule; when a personal hearing is / isn't
  required (Abdus Salam line, SCJ-032); malice in law (SCJ-126).
- **§4. Interpretive & equitable maxims deployed against/for licensees.** No advantage of
  one's own wrong; approbate-and-reprobate (`approbate-and-reprobate-bars-selective-
  repudiation`); acquiescence-cuts-both-ways; res judicata (`CPC::res-judicata`, SCJ-031);
  noscitur a sociis / purposive construction (SCJ-133, SCJ-147).

### PART IX — Master Table of Authorities  · `09-table-of-authorities.md`
- The full 330-authority citation ledger — the project's explicit "master citation ledger"
  aim. Organised: (a) **Supreme Court authorities** by subject (arrears; s.126 assessment;
  delegated-legislation vires; natural justice; consumer-forum/tribunal; tariff/regulatory);
  (b) **High Court authorities** likewise; (c) index of which corpus cases cite each, with
  treatment (followed / distinguished / applied). Generated from `authorities_ledger`, then
  edited into subject clusters. The 28 multiply-cited authorities headline each cluster.

### PART X — Register of Open Questions ("Not Decided")  · `10-open-questions.md`
- The 77 `not_decided` points, grouped by theme (assessment quantum left to the authority;
  landlord-tenant relationship left to civil court; captive/open-access surcharge; etc.),
  each with the case that expressly left it open — a map of where the Supply Code
  jurisprudence is still unsettled.

Double the sample, and the headline flips

At five orders the U.P. Electricity Ombudsman looked like a pure gatekeeper. Five more — mostly merits wins for consumers — turn that into 6 relief / 4 threshold. The durable finding isn't a number; it's that a confident pattern from five rows was wrong by row ten.

The clearest doctrine: assessment on periodic-check failure

Two near-identical KESCO/Kanpur orders (OMB-007, OMB-008) make a repeatable, citable rule — the strongest jurisprudence signal in the set.

Rule: where the licensee breaches its duty to check the meter periodically — Supply Code 2005 cl. 5.5(b) (every 1–2 years) — and thereby dumps a delayed assessment on the consumer, the Ombudsman corrects the tariff / reduces the assessment, allows payment in 5 interest-free installments, and directs action against the officials and replacement of the meter. Two matching applications in one batch = a pattern worth citing.

The ladder is busier than it looked

Six of the ten are appeals from — or enforcement of — CGRF orders. The Ombudsman both polices the CGRF's competence (OMB-010) and enforces its awards (OMB-003, 006, 010).

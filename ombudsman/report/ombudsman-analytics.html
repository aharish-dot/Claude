Five orders in, the pattern is already about the door — not the merits

A first read of five 2026 orders of the Electricity Ombudsman, Uttar Pradesh, coded on what each dispute was about and how it was actually decided. The gap between those two is the finding.

n = 5 · orders C-19, C-20, C-25, 23 & 15 of 2026 · a demonstration of the coding, not a statistic

4/5disposed at the threshold

Four of the five never reached the electricity merits. They were turned away on a jurisdiction or maintainability question — theft is outside the forum, the Ombudsman can't review its own order, exhaust the CGRF first, or the appeal was withdrawn.

The one order that granted relief did so by enforcing a lower forum's order under s.142 — not by deciding a fresh dispute. So in this sample the Ombudsman reads as a gatekeeper of its own jurisdiction.

theft → barred no power of review exhaust CGRF first withdrawn enforcement (s.142)

The disputes were all different. The outcome wasn't.

Five distinct subject-matters across five orders — which is exactly why coding the procedural axis matters: a subject-only sheet would file four dismissals-at-the-door under "metering / billing / supply" and miss the through-line.

01Assessment — theft / unauthorised use. s.126/135 territory → outside the forum.
02Metering — defective meter, kVAH assessment. reached only as a review petition.
03Billing — electricity-duty exemption. the lone relief — by enforcement.
04Franchisee / single-point over-recovery. "no profit, no loss" — cl. 4.46(b)(i).
05Supply reliability / hours → compensation. routed back to CGRF / SoP Regs 2019.

Where these sit on the forum ladder

The Ombudsman keeps pointing down. Its recurring move is to insist it is appellate-only and send the consumer back to the CGRF — which is why "exhaust the remedy below" is the doctrine to watch.

The Representationist Sri Alok Kumar @ Alok Sharma, in its Representation dated

“The present Appeal/Representation is being preferred under Section 42(6) of the
Electricity Act, 2003 read with the U.P. Electricity Regulatory Commission (Consumer
Grievance Redressal Forum and Electricity Ombudsman) Regulations, 2022 against the
impugned order dated 25.04.2026 passed by the Ld. Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum
(CGRF), Loni, whereby the complaint preferred by the Appellant has been dismissed solely on
the erroneous, hyper-technical, and legally unsustainable ground that the Appellant allegedly
does not fall within the definition of "consumer" under Section 2(15) of the Electricity Act,
2003. The impugned order is arbitrary, contrary to the statutory scheme of the Electricity Act,
2003, inconsistent with the U.P. Electricity Supply Code, 2005 and the applicable Tariff Orders
issued by the Uttar Pradesh Electricity Regulatory Commission, and suffers from complete
non-application of mind inasmuch as the Ld. CGRF failed to adjudicate the substantive
grievances raised by the Appellant concerning illegal electricity recovery, unauthorized levies,
non-transparent billing practices, and regulatory violations committed by Respondent No(s). 1
and 2 while operating the prepaid electricity distribution mechanism within OXY Homes
Society. The appellant Sh. Alok Kumar @ Alok Sharma husband of Akanksha Sharma (the
owner) and 'Karta' of the family, is a permanent resident of Flat No. A-508, OXY Homes
Society, Bhopura, Ghaziabad and has been continuously residing therein along with his wife
and family members since the year 2016. The electricity supplied to the said premises is
continuously used, consumed, and enjoyed by the Appellant and his family members for
domestic purposes and the Appellant is the actual user, beneficiary, and end-consumer of the
electricity supply connected with the said premises. Documentary records including electricity
bills, prepaid meter statements, maintenance invoices, bank records, and other material placed
before the Ld. CGRF clearly establish the Appellant's continuous residence, beneficial use, and
financial liability concerning the electricity supply connected with Flat No. A-508. The
Appellant had approached the Ld. CGRF raising serious grievances concerning excessive
electricity charges recovery, inflated fixed charges, excessive unit rates, unauthorized DG
charges, arbitrary penalties, illegal recovery of maintenance and other non-electricity charges

through prepaid electricity billing systems, lack of transparency in billing practices, and non-

disclosure of electricity audit records by Respondent No(s). 1 and 2. The Appellant had
specifically pleaded before the Ld. CGRF that Respondent No(s). 1 and 2, while operating the
prepaid electricity distribution mechanism under the guise of a deemed franchisee/single-point

recovering DG charges, maintenance charges, AMC charges, vending charges, penalties, and

systems despite there being no statutory sanction permitting such coercive recovery through

electricity procurement records, billing calculations, statements of recovery, reconciled
accounts, audit reports, and other financial records concerning the prepaid electricity
distribution mechanism operating within OXY Homes Society and had specifically prayed for
an independent forensic/electricity audit in order to ascertain the actual amount collected from
residents vis-a-vis the amount paid to the distribution licensee. The grievances raised by the
Appellant were not isolated or individual in nature. Several similarly situated residents of OXY
Homes Society had independently submitted written complaints and representations before the
Ld. CGRF raising substantially identical grievances concerning excessive electricity recovery.
inflated fixed charges, illegal DG charges, arbitrary prepaid billing practices, unauthorized
levies, and non-disclosure of electricity audit records. At least three other residents had also
filed complaints raising materially similar allegations against Respondent No(s). 1 and 2. The
existence of multiple complaints concerning the same prepaid electricity distribution
mechanism clearly established a recurring and systemic pattern of statutory and regulatory
violations affecting a large body of residents and consumers within the society. During the
proceedings, the Ld. CGRF itself acknowledged that under Clause 4.46 (b) (i) of the U.P.
Electricity Supply Code, 2005, a builder/deemed franchisee cannot derive profit from
electricity distribution and can recover electricity charges only on a "no profit no loss basis."
The Ld. Forum further acknowledged that fixed charges recovered from residents cannot
exceed the charges actually paid to the electricity distribution licensee and that deemed
franchisees are under statutory obligations to maintain transparency and audited accounts under

the applicable UPERC Tariff Orders. Despite recording such findings and acknowledging the

existence of serious statutory and regulatory issues, the Ld. Forum failed to adjudicate the
legality of the billing and recovery mechanism adopted by Respondent No(s). 1 and 2 and failed
to grant any effective or consequential relief in favour of the Appellant. Instead, the complaint
was dismissed solely on the hyper-technical ground that the Appellant is not a "consumer"
under Section 2 (15) of the Electricity Act, 2003. The Ld. CGRF gravely erred in holding that
the Appellant is not a "consumer" merely because the premises or related records stand in the
name of the Appellant's wife. The Ld. Forum failed to appreciate that the definition of
"consumer" under Section 2 (15) of the Electricity Act, 2003 is broad, inclusive, and beneficial
in nature and extends to every actual user and beneficiary of electricity supply. The Appellant,
being the husband of the recorded owner, continuous resident of the premises since 2016, actual
user and beneficiary of electricity supply, and person responsible for payment of electricity
charges and related dues, squarely falls within the ambit and scope of "consumer" under
Section 2(15) of the Electricity Act, 2003. The impugned order is contrary to the law laid down
in Hari Chand v. Haryana Vidyut Prasaran Nigam Ltd. wherein the Hon'ble National Consumer
Disputes Redressal Commission recognized that an actual user and beneficiary of electricity
supply is entitled to maintain proceedings concerning electricity disputes even where the
electricity connection stands in the name of another person. The Ld. Forum failed to appreciate
that electricity consumption in a domestic household is collective in nature and the benefit of
electricity supply is enjoyed by all members residing in the premises. The statutory protection
available under the Electricity Act cannot be denied to an actual user merely because the
electricity connection or property records stand in the name of another family member. The
impugned order further suffers from complete non-application of mind, jurisdictional error,
abdication of statutory duties, and failure to exercise the powers vested in the Ld. CGRF under
Section 42 of the Electricity Act, 2003. Having acknowledged the statutory obligations
governing deemed franchisees and the allegations concerning illegal recoveries and non-
transparent billing practices, the Ld. Forum could not have dismissed the complaint solely on
a technical objection relating to maintainability. The present dispute squarely falls within the
statutory grievance redressal mechanism established under Section 42 of the Electricity Act,
2003 and therefore required adjudication on merits by the Ld. CGRF. No other appeal,
representation, writ petition, suit, or proceeding challenging the impugned order dated
25.04.2026 is pending before any Court, Tribunal, Regulatory Authority, or any other
competent Forum. The present Representation/Appeal is within limitation and is fully

maintainable under Section 42 (6) of the Electricity Act, 2003 read with the applicable UPERC
